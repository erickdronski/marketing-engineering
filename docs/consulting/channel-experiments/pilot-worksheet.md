# Channel pilot worksheet

Use one copy per decision. Fill in the design with the operator before launch, and fill in the readout after the outcome window closes. Save approved assets in the client’s workspace. Keep a link from each reported number to the export that produced it.

| Before launch | Client entry |
|---|---|
| Decision | Who will change what if the pilot works? What is the alternative? |
| Audience and comparison | Eligible people or accounts, allocation or comparison period, exclusions, and other changes that might affect the result. |
| Variable | Channel, campaign ID, asset or page version, one planned change and what stays fixed. |
| Outcome | Event, unit counted, denominator, duplicate rule, attribution window, time zone, source owner and expected reporting delay. |
| Costs and controls | Media, creator fees, rights, production and staff time. Name the budget cap, permitted action, reviewer, and stop condition. |
| Decision rule | Minimum data quality and volume, acceptable cost or other target from the client’s economics, and the date the owner will review it. |

| At review | Client entry |
|---|---|
| Observations | Actual dates, spend and fees, numerator, denominator, cost per outcome, unmatched records and sources checked. Leave an undefined rate empty. |
| Interpretation | What could also explain the difference? Which exposures are merely observed? What has yet to mature? |
| Decision and owner | Repeat, revise, stop, or hold for data. Record the reason, owner, next date and client deliverables. |

## Worked decision for a home-services business

A fictional owner is choosing which creator clip to test a second time. A is a before-and-after video. B demonstrates what happens during an estimate. Both feature the same service, area, offer and landing form. Each link carries its own campaign ID into dispatch. The owner authorizes a $480 first test. In this example, the fees cover production and usage, and there are no other pilot costs. The client’s target is at most $75 per booked estimate and at least ten bookings per clip before increasing the budget.

| Recorded outcome | Clip A | Clip B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Media spend plus creator fee | $180 + $60 | $180 + $60 |
| Clicks | 120 | 60 |
| Distinct eligible quote requests | 10 | 8 |
| Requests with a booked estimate within 7 days | 2 | 4 |
| Booking rate among eligible requests | 20% | 50% |
| Pilot cost per booked estimate | $120 | $60 |

The dispatcher counted each request once, reconciled all 18 IDs and waited seven days after the last request. B met the cost target on four bookings. The owner records **test B again at a cap of $240 if a second pilot is approved**. Both clips fell short of the ten-booking threshold for expansion. The counts describe bookings attributed to these links. Audience mix could explain part of the difference. A balanced or randomized comparison would make a stronger case for shifting spend. Record completed jobs and margin separately before claiming a profit.
