# Creator content, Meta ads and LinkedIn

Pick one decision for the next review. A creator pilot might decide which demonstration to license. A Meta pilot might decide whether to fund another week of ads. A LinkedIn pilot might decide which guide to sponsor. Give each creative version, its destination link and the resulting campaign a stable ID so the reviewer can trace a booking back to the work.

## Creator content people can recognize

Search public TikTok posts for the buyer’s problem and a few competing products. Record the format, opening question, product demonstration and date for several examples, including ones with little engagement. Label a paid relationship only where it is disclosed. A popular before-and-after format gives you an idea to test with your own footage.

Give two creators the same brief and let each show how they solve the problem in their own words. Ask them to name the product when it first appears and demonstrate one observable result. Turn a recurring viewer question into the next brief. Get permission before featuring a viewer’s words or likeness.

**Deliver a rights and cost sheet.** Record the creator fee, delivery date, disclosure, approved edit, channels, paid usage period and renewal owner. If an agreement includes a performance bonus, define the metric, window, cap and treatment of paid views. Price paid reuse explicitly, including any agreed share of spend. A creator should be able to tell what they will earn before filming. Keep the endorsement disclosure with the content when republishing. [FTC guidance for US creator disclosures](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)

Give organic posts and licensed ads separate links. Record views for reach and deduplicated purchases or bookings for the business decision. In a fictional local pilot, $120 in creator fees plus $180 in media produced six distinct booked estimates matched to the campaign after the booking window closed. That is **$50 per matched booking**. Keep unattributed bookings visible. Measuring how many bookings the ad added requires a comparison group. ## Meta ads with a traceable outcome

1. **Test delivery before creative.** In a test flow, follow one tagged click to a confirmed purchase or booking. Reconcile its event ID to the order or booking record. If both browser and server report it, verify it counts once. Test both consent choices against the client’s tracking settings, then repeat after a form, checkout or tracking release. Exclude test records from results.
2. **Choose the event and constraint.** Optimize for the deepest reliable event the client can measure at sufficient volume. If bookings are sparse, use a qualified inquiry as an interim event and report the eventual booking rate. Agree on maximum spend, a cost the business can sustain and a review date. A named person authorizes the spend. [Action permissions](../../permissions.md)
3. **Change one thing you can explain.** Test a phone demonstration against a polished cut of the same task, or compare two customer problems while holding the format and offer steady. A role-specific headline is a message hypothesis. Check who actually responds. Use an AI reader’s critique to revise an unclear offer before filming. Evaluate the result with customers.
4. **Decide when to refresh.** Review frequency, spend and mature qualified outcomes together on a regular schedule. If repeat exposure rises while cost per outcome deteriorates, try a new demonstration. If tracking failed or too few outcomes matured, fix the data or record an inconclusive result before authorizing more spend.

## LinkedIn guides that reach buyers

Start with a question from a sales call. Give the answer a descriptive title and publish a guide the buyer can use without a demo. Save the actual permalink and check it after any headline change. Compare it with older posts that still answer the same question. Recheck claims and offer dates before circulating an older guide. Use LinkedIn’s ad library to see what propositions peers are testing. The library identifies the creative and sponsor. Verify commercial outcomes separately. [LinkedIn Ad Library](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1517918)

Look for useful replies from buyers before paying to extend a guide’s reach. For an eligible customer or creator post, get the author’s platform permission, document the usage agreement and submit the specific post for review. For an acquisition pilot, define how to handle staff, current customers and competitors using audience controls the client can legitimately apply. For an expansion pilot, existing customers may be the intended audience. [LinkedIn thought leader ads](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a1450002) · [Review queue](../../review-queue.md)

**Deliver an opportunity review.** Count distinct CRM opportunity IDs with a traced campaign click. Alongside that number, report deals at accounts with eligible recorded ad exposure during a stated lookback window, when that reporting is available. Label the second measure as account-level exposure, and show how many deals overlap. Report click-through rate separately. A guide with few clicks may merit another test if relevant accounts engage, but a deal at an exposed account alone cannot establish the ad’s effect. Use a comparable holdout when the decision requires an estimate of incremental pipeline.
