# Follow one decision

A dispatcher looking at a full job board is the audience for the Wrenfield example. The fictional product owner wants to ship a route-planning announcement. The brief makes the feature page a dependency so readers have somewhere to go when the post appears.

1. The owner records the launch goal and the source used to check delivery.
2. The orchestrator assigns the feature page to the search owner and the post to content. Research supplies a signed claim for a specific time study.
3. The content owner submits a draft. The reviewer asks for an opening grounded in the dispatcher's day and records why.
4. The revised item enters a new review. Approval binds the copy and its action. The operator checks the exact version before delivery.
5. After delivery the operator records a receipt. Tracking can then link an outcome to the release and report coverage, missing campaign IDs and the age of the data. A new brief can use the result and the saved edit.
